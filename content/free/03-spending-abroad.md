Two silent costs eat holiday money: **foreign transaction fees** from your bank and **bad exchange rates** from card machines. Both are easy to avoid.

## 1. Get a card with no overseas fees
Many standard UK current accounts add a fee every time you spend or withdraw abroad. Plenty of cards don't. As of **October 2026**, MoneySavingExpert's travel card round-up highlights options such as:
- **Debit:** First Direct and Chase (no fees on overseas spending), and Starling is also often recommended by Which?.
- **Credit:** Lloyds Ultra (no overseas spending or cash machine fees, per MSE).

These change, so check the current [MoneySavingExpert travel cards guide](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/credit-cards/travel-credit-cards/) before applying. We're not paid by any of them.

**Credit card tip:** paying for bigger things (like a package holiday) on a credit card can give you Section 75 protection. Only do it if you'll clear the balance in full, or the interest wipes out the saving.

## 2. Always choose the local currency
A card machine, shop or cash machine abroad may ask if you want to pay in pounds. Say **no**. This is called dynamic currency conversion: the machine picks the rate, and it's usually worse than your card's. Choose euros (or the local currency) every time.

## 3. Cash machines
- Use a bank's own machine where you can.
- Avoid stand-alone "cash point" machines in tourist areas, which often charge their own fee.
- Again, decline the conversion and take cash in local currency.

## 4. Don't buy currency at the airport
Airport bureaux usually have the worst rates. If you want some cash on you, order online in advance for collection or just withdraw on arrival with a fee-free card.

## 5. Tap to pay is your friend
In the Netherlands, for example, you can tap a contactless bank card on the trains: NS lists Schiphol to Amsterdam Centraal at **€5.50** second class with OVpay contactless, versus **€7.10** with a single-use chip card *(NS 2026 price list)*. That's a saving just for using your card.
