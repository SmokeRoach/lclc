🧳 The SmokeRoach Starter: 10 money savers we use on every trip

The basics we wish someone had told us before our first cheap trip. None of it is secret, all of it saves real money.

✋ Straight up

You could do all of this yourself

Every saver on this site is something you could work out on your own with enough time and patience. We're Scott and Charlotte (plus Gunner) from Liverpool, and we've trial-and-errored it across loads of trips to find the best possible price. You're paying for our shortcuts, not secrets.

🇬🇧 For British travellers only. Everything is tested for getting the best price as a UK traveller, from UK airports, in pounds. We can't speak for other countries.

None of this is rocket science. You could find every one of these yourself. We just learned most of them the expensive way, so you don't have to.

1. Check in online. Always.

Ryanair now runs on digital boarding passes in its app. If you turn up without checking in online, Ryanair's fees page says you pay an airport check-in fee: £55 per person, per flight (£30 on flights from Spain), unless you're on a fare that includes it. (Ryanair fees page, checked Oct 2026.) Set a reminder for when check-in opens.

2. Know your free bag size before you pack

The free under-seat bag is different on every airline. Ryanair's is 40 × 30 × 20 cm, easyJet's is 45 × 36 × 20 cm. Get it wrong and you pay at the gate. Full breakdown in our free bag fees guide.

3. Use the free drop-off, not the pricey one

At Liverpool John Lennon, the Express Drop-off right outside the terminal is £7 for up to 10 minutes (from 1 Sept 2026). Drop-off 2 is free for 20 minutes and about a seven-minute walk. (liverpoolairport.com, Oct 2026.) Manchester has a free option too: JetParks 1, free for up to an hour, with a shuttle bus. (manchesterairport.co.uk, Oct 2026.)

4. Or skip the car and get the bus

The Arriva 500 runs between Liverpool city centre and the airport. Arriva lists an adult single at £2 (Oct 2026). That's less than the drop-off fee.

5. Pay with a card that doesn't charge foreign fees

Lots of normal UK bank cards add a fee every time you spend abroad. A fee-free card fixes that for good. See our free spending money abroad guide.

6. Always pay in the local currency

When a card machine or cash machine abroad asks "pay in pounds or euros?", pick euros (or whatever the local currency is). Choosing pounds lets the machine set the exchange rate, and it's rarely in your favour.

7. Get your free GHIC

The UK Global Health Insurance Card is free from the official NHS site. Ignore any website that charges you for it. It doesn't replace travel insurance, though (see the free before you go checklist).

8. Supermarket first, restaurant second

Our first spend in Amsterdam on the £99 challenge was Albert Heijn: a Coke for 45p and crisps for 65p (our own receipts, Sept 2026). Breakfast and snacks from a supermarket free up money for one proper meal.

9. Bring an empty water bottle

You can't take liquids over 100 ml through most security lanes, but an empty bottle is fine. Fill it airside at a water fountain and skip buying airport-priced water.

10. Write your budget down before you go

Every one of our price challenges starts with a number. Decide yours, split it into travel / getting around / food / fun, and check it halfway through the day. Watch us do it in the £99 Amsterdam day trip (budget check at 27:26).
