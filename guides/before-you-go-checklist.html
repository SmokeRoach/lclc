🛂 Before you go: the UK traveller's checklist for Europe (Oct 2026)

EES fingerprints, ETIAS, GHIC, insurance and ATOL. What's actually changed for Brits in 2026, and what you need to do about it.

✋ Straight up

You could do all of this yourself

Every saver on this site is something you could work out on your own with enough time and patience. We're Scott and Charlotte (plus Gunner) from Liverpool, and we've trial-and-errored it across loads of trips to find the best possible price. You're paying for our shortcuts, not secrets.

🇬🇧 For British travellers only. Everything is tested for getting the best price as a UK traveller, from UK airports, in pounds. We can't speak for other countries.

This is the stuff that costs you money (or a missed trip) if you get it wrong. Everything below was checked against official sources in October 2026.

1. EES: the new border fingerprints (live now)

The EU's Entry/Exit System has been fully operational since 10 April 2026. As a UK passport holder visiting for a short stay, you'll have your face and fingerprints registered the first time you cross into the Schengen area, and passport stamps are replaced. (EU travel-europe.europa.eu, Oct 2026.) Leave extra time at the border, especially on coaches and ferries.

2. ETIAS: not live yet, don't pay anyone

ETIAS is the EU's upcoming online travel authorisation for visa-free visitors, including Brits. The official site says it is not in operation and no applications are being taken yet. The official fee will be €20. Scam sites already exist, so only ever apply through the official EU site once it opens.

3. The 90/180 rule

You can stay up to 90 days in any 180 across the Schengen countries combined. EES now tracks it automatically.

4. Passport

For most EU countries your passport must have been issued less than 10 years before the day you arrive and be valid for at least 3 months after the day you plan to leave. Check both dates before you book anything. The full rules for travelling to the EU on a UK passport are on GOV.UK foreign travel advice for each country.

5. GHIC: free, apply on the NHS site

The UK Global Health Insurance Card gets you medically necessary state healthcare in the EU. It's free on the official NHS site. The NHS is clear it's not a substitute for travel insurance.

6. Travel insurance basics

  • Buy it when you book, not the night before. That way you're covered if you have to cancel.
  • Check the medical cover, cancellation cover and the excess.
  • Declare medical conditions. Undeclared conditions can void a claim.
  • Going away more than once a year? An annual policy is often cheaper than two single-trip ones.
  • Compare on a comparison site, then read the policy wording of the one you pick.

7. ATOL: why we like package holidays

If you book a flight-inclusive package with an ATOL-protected company and it goes bust, ATOL protects your money: a refund before you travel, or help getting home if you're already away. It isn't travel insurance: it doesn't cover illness, missed flights or lost luggage. Check for the ATOL and keep your ATOL Certificate. (ATOL / UK CAA, Oct 2026.)

8. Cannabis and borders (keep it simple)

It's illegal in the UK. Never bring anything back, not even "leftovers". In Amsterdam, smoking it is banned in public areas of the centre, including the Red Light District. Our travel tips come first; please follow local law.
